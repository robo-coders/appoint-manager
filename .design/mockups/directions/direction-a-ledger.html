Where the money lands

Deposits are not much use to you if they sit in somebody else's balance until somebody else releases them.

The Stripe account is in your name.
You open it during setup — Stripe Express, your salon, your bank details. We are not the merchant on your clients' statements and we never hold their money.
Deposits arrive in your bank, not ours.
Not into a balance we control and release on a schedule we chose. Refunds are yours to give, from your own account, without asking us first.
Your clients are yours.
Names, dogs, breeds, appointment history — all of it exports to a spreadsheet whenever you want. There is no marketplace and no pet-owner app, so nobody is shown three other groomers on the way to booking with you.
One booking page, and it is yours.
Your name, your services, your hours, your colour. Not a profile inside somebody else's directory.

How you leave

We would rather write this down now than have you find it out later.

There is no contract and no notice period. It is a monthly subscription and you end it in settings, in one place, without emailing anybody. Your client list and your appointment history export to a spreadsheet on the way out. The Stripe account leaves with you, because it was always yours.

What you cannot take is the booking page itself. That lives here, and if you go, the link stops working — so keep your own note of where you sent your clients. Everything you brought, you keep.

The questions we actually get

Including the two that go badly for us.

Tuft is free and it already does deposits. Why would I pay £39?
Because their free plan is funded by a booking fee on your clients and ours is funded by you. At eighty appointments a month that is around £100 of their money against £39 of yours. If your book is quiet — twenty appointments, say — the free plan is genuinely cheaper for everyone involved and we will not pretend otherwise. Deposits are not the reason to come here. Who pays for them is.
What if they stop charging my clients?
They have said publicly that they are looking at letting groomers absorb the fee instead. If that lands, the bill at the top of this page stops being an argument, and you would be choosing on the rest of it: your own booking page, your own Stripe account, your own client list, and nobody's marketplace standing between you and the people who already know you. We would rather you knew that argument had an expiry date.
Will asking for a deposit lose me clients?
The ones who vanish on a Saturday might. A medium full groom averages £55 in the UK. Three no-shows a week is £165, and £660 over a month. A £10 hold is not a punishment: it comes off the bill on the day, and it is how the book stays honest.
What happens to the book I already have?
We import names, dogs and the next fortnight from a spreadsheet before your booking page goes live, so you are not starting from an empty diary. For the first ten salons we come and do it with you.
